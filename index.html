111, Inc. · Nasdaq: YI · Shanghai

Supplying China’s pharmacies online, mid-transition

111, Inc. sells medicines and health products to pharmacies through its online wholesale platform 1药城 and to shoppers through 1药网. Sales to pharmacies were 97.5% of FY2025 revenue. This page reads the FY2025 annual report and the 2026 filings: what it sells, what it earned, what it owes its investors, and the proposal now before its board.

500,000+

pharmacies served since the May 2017 launch of the wholesale platform, across 31 provinces, as of Dec 31, 2025

The 20-F says “more than 500,000” and adds that it sources directly from more than 490 pharmaceutical companies. These are the company’s own counts, not audited. 20-F, Apr 30, 2026

Mostly a wholesaler to pharmacies

111 buys medicines and health products from pharmaceutical companies and distributors and sells them on through two apps and websites. Under its direct sales model it takes the stock and is responsible for fulfilment and delivery; third-party sellers can also sell through its marketplaces. The larger platform serves pharmacies. The smaller one serves shoppers. 20-F, Apr 30, 2026

Three platforms, three names for each

  1. 1药城 online wholesale pharmacy, B2B

    A one-stop shop for pharmacies, clinics and hospitals to source pharmaceutical products, on an app and a website. The 20-F counts more than 500,000 pharmacies served since the May 2017 launch and describes the customers as small and medium retail chains, independent pharmacies and in-house pharmacies of clinics and private hospitals. Named “1 Pharmacy” in the 20-F. 20-F, Apr 30, 2026

  2. 1药网 online retail pharmacy, B2C

    Launched by the founders in 2010, one of the first online retail pharmacies in China, the 20-F says. It sells under a direct model and, since 2016, through a marketplace of third-party sellers that pay commissions and annual platform fees. It also runs branded storefronts on Tmall.com, JD.com and PDD.com. Named “1 Medicine Marketplace” in the 20-F, and “1 Pharmacy” in the company’s September 2026 releases. 20-F, Apr 30, 2026 proposal 6-K, Sep 17, 2026

  3. 1诊 internet hospital

    The company’s September 17, 2026 release describes it as an internet hospital offering online consultation, electronic prescriptions and patient management. The 20-F does not name it. It mentions online consultation and e-prescription services among its value-added services, and no revenue line is reported for it, so its size is not known from the filings. It is also listed as a brand on the company’s own corporate website, which is in Chinese. proposal 6-K, Sep 17, 2026 20-F, Apr 30, 2026

How the money is made

  • Direct sales. It buys from pharmaceutical companies or distributors and sells to pharmacies or shoppers, carrying the inventory. In FY2025 the cost of products sold was RMB 11,832.6 million of RMB 12,556.0 million of revenue.
  • Marketplace. Third-party sellers list on the platforms; 111 processes the orders and collects commissions, a share of sales that depends on category, and a fixed annual platform fee. Marketplace service revenue was RMB 56.7 million.
  • Services to drug makers. Marketing, channel data and supply-chain services. Service revenue in total was RMB 85.4 million in FY2025, down 19.8% from FY2024.

Why sales are falling

The 20-F gives two reasons for the 12.8% fall in FY2025 revenue: “enhanced competition in the pharmaceutical retail and wholesale industry in China”, and the move to an asset-light model after it sold three subsidiaries in the third quarter of 2025. Those three pharmacy companies (in Shanxi, Liaoning and Tianjin) made up about 20.0% of consolidated net revenue in 2024 and now serve 111 as fulfilment partners. 20-F, Apr 30, 2026

The 20-F names traditional distributors, a couple of B2B platforms, offline pharmacies and online platforms such as Ali Health and JD.com as competitors. It also says the company believes no comparable company competes with it across the whole pharmaceutical value chain, which is its own view. No market-size figure is given in the filings, so none is shown here. 20-F, Apr 30, 2026

What is owed to investors, and what is held

In 2020 investors put RMB 419.82 million and RMB 515 million into 111’s operating subsidiary, 1 Pharmacy Technology (Shanghai), and agreed to take all necessary and reasonable steps to help it list on Shanghai’s STAR Market. If the listing had not been completed, and the China Securities Regulatory Commission had not otherwise approved the registration application, by June 30, 2023, they could ask the company’s Hong Kong subsidiary, Yao Wang, to buy back their equity at cost plus 6% a year. Certain of the investors have since made redemption requests under those terms, the 20-F says. 20-F, Apr 30, 2026

How to read the bars. Owed is the amount recorded under redeemable non-controlling interests and under accrued expenses and other current liabilities for the 1 Pharmacy Technology investors. Held is cash and cash equivalents, restricted cash and short-term investments, the company’s own total in the Q2 release; restricted cash is not freely available. The rights belong to the investors, and the money falls due only if they exercise them. At Dec 31, 2025 the 20-F’s table by jurisdiction puts RMB 518.7 million of the RMB 561.0 million of cash and short-term investments (restricted cash excluded) in the PRC, RMB 41.0 million in the Cayman Islands and RMB 1.3 million in Hong Kong (summed here from the table). The investors’ right runs against Yao Wang, the company’s Hong Kong subsidiary. The company has signed agreements or commitments with all of them to restructure the obligations through phased repayments over extended periods. At the date of the 20-F, investors holding 57.15% of the remaining principal had agreed to extend the due dates by another year; the Q1 release says investors representing 60.3% of the outstanding principal had agreed to restructure at extended periods, and the Q2 release says 63.8%. The Q1 release also reported RMB 396.6 million held and RMB 0.95 billion owed at Mar 31, 2026. On the Jun 30 bars the hatched outline repeats the Dec 31 length of the same bar, so the change reads along one scale. The auditor’s reports on the FY2025 and FY2023 accounts each carry a going-concern paragraph that names this exposure, and each says the statements present fairly, in all material respects. The bars show the filed figures and are not a statement of solvency. 20-F, Apr 30, 2026 Q2 2026 results 6-K, Sep 17, 2026 Q1 2026 results 6-K, Jun 4, 2026 Q4 and FY2025 6-K, Apr 9, 2026

A take-private proposal from the holders of the votes

The two co-founders, who hold every Class B share, and a sponsor have proposed to buy the Class A shares they do not already own. The filings describe a proposal and a process. They do not describe a decision.

The proposal, Sep 16, 2026

From
Dr. Gang Yu and Mr. Junling Liu, co-founders and co-chairmen (Mr. Liu is also chief executive officer), and Huadeng Tech BioArray Ventures Ltd, the sponsor.
Price
US$0.226 per Class A share, or US$4.52 per ADS, in cash. One ADS represents twenty Class A shares.
Premium
29.5% to the ADS closing price on Sep 15, 2026, and 20% to the average closing price over the previous 60 trading days, according to the letter.
Funding
Equity capital: rollover equity from the co-founders and cash from the sponsor. The letter says the proposal would not be subject to any debt-financing uncertainty or delay and has no financing condition, and expects definitive commitments for the financing to be in place when the definitive documents are signed.
Votes
By a consortium agreement the co-founders have agreed to vote all their shares in favour. The Schedule 13D says that for six months after the agreement is signed (subject to extension or early termination) the consortium members work exclusively together, vote against any competing transaction, and do not make a competing proposal or acquire or dispose of any securities of the company.
Gate
The co-founders say they will not move forward unless a special committee of independent and disinterested directors approves, and that directors affiliated with them will not take part.
Status
“Preliminary non-binding”. The board said on Sep 17 that it had not yet evaluated it. On Sep 28 it formed a special committee of Jian Sun (chair), Nee Chuan Teo and Jun Luo, which will also consider “other potential strategic alternatives”, and said no decisions had been made.

What this page does not settle

Choices made in reading the filings, and what was left out.

  1. Currency. The reporting currency is the renminbi. The US dollar figures in the filings are convenience translations at the rate on the balance-sheet date (about RMB 6.993 per US$ for FY2025; the Q2 2026 release uses about RMB 6.785). Figures here are in RMB unless marked US$, and the millions are derived from the reported thousands.
  2. Names. 111’s own documents use different English names for the same businesses. The 20-F calls 1药网 “1 Medicine Marketplace” and 1药城 “1 Pharmacy”; the September 2026 releases call the retail pharmacy “1 Pharmacy” and the B2B platform “1 Medicine”; the investor relations site still says “1 Drugstore” and “1 Drug Mall”, which the 20-F gives as their earlier names. This page leads with the Chinese names.
  3. Website claims. The corporate website, in Chinese, says the company is a leader in China’s internet medicine and health sector, that it was the first such company to list in the United States, and that it follows an S2B2C model. The first-to-list and S2B2C claims are not in the filings. The filings use “leading” and “early mover” language about the company’s own position, which is the company’s view. None of these claims is verified here. Corporate site
  4. Left out. The live share quote on the investor relations site, its listing of third-party research, its stock imagery, and the photographs of partnership signings, which show named people and other companies. Directors appear with the roles, ages and background the 20-F gives, and without portraits.
  5. Nasdaq dates. The 20-F gives the start of Nasdaq listing as Sep 12, 2018 in Item 9 and Sep 15, 2018 in Item 4. This page says September 2018.
  6. ADS counts. One ADS has represented twenty Class A shares since Jan 24, 2025 and two before. Per-ADS figures before and after that date are not comparable.
  7. Derived figures. Shares of revenue, segment margins, the share of owed obligations held, the bar lengths and the million-RMB figures are computed here from reported numbers and are labelled where they appear. The US$ translations are the company’s.
  8. Not covered. The 20-F’s discussion of PRC drug-price policy and centralized procurement, the company’s use of home-country practice in place of some Nasdaq governance rules, the full June 30, 2026 balance sheet in the Q2 release, and the director and officer share transactions reported on Form 4, which are on the EDGAR filing list. Read these in the filings.
  9. As of. The latest filing reviewed is the 6-K of Sep 28, 2026. Anything after that, including the response to the proposal, is not known to this page.
